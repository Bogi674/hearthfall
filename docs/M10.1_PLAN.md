# M10.1 Plan: The Living House

Status: plan only. No code changed yet.
Replaces the M10 scaffold (commit 65688a5) with a house that works end to end.

## 1. Goal

The house is the heart of the game. The player builds it floor by floor, wall by wall, room by room. Colonists build it, furnish it, live in it, and defend it. The day loop stays the same: work outside by day, come home at dusk, rest and prepare at night. The perimeter wall, lookout, and watchtower gameplay stays. The airship is staged from the house.

## 2. Assumptions and conflicts to confirm

1. **Tile based building, like The Sims.** The player paints floors, draws wall segments, places doors, then places furniture. A room is any enclosed floor area. This replaces the M10 prefab rooms and the M8 room buildings. It conflicts with `GAME_DESIGN.md` section 5.5, which I will rewrite.
2. **Mingle.** Section 17 puts mood and relationships out of scope. I will add mingling as a hope bonus and an animation only. No relationship state.
3. **Saves.** Version 4. Version 3 saves are rejected, as v1 was at M7.
4. **Perimeter stays outside the house.** Workshop, Smelter, Sawmill, Kitchen, and the rest stay compound buildings. The house gets indoor furniture versions of cooking, crafting, and storage.
5. **The house lot shrinks from the M10 values.** Lot radius 3, 4, 5, 6, 7 per hearth stage (7 by 7 up to 15 by 15). M10's 11 pushed normal buildings off the map and broke the full run.

## 3. Architecture

The house is a layer of plain data in `World`. No parallel Room objects.

| Data | Shape | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `house.floors` | `Record<tileIndex, FloorId>` | Wood, stone, rug base. Marks a tile as inside. |
| `house.edges` | `HouseEdge[]` | `{ id, x, y, side: 'n' or 'w', kind, level, hp, construct }`. Kinds: wall, door, window, gunPort. Levels: wood, reinforced, stone, metal. |
| Furniture | `Building` entries with `furniture: true` | Reuses construction, hp, light, saves, and selection. Adds `facing` 0 to 3. |
| Roof pads | Furniture type `roofPad` | Allowed only on a floor tile with a wall edge on its outer side. |
| Derived rooms | Computed by flood fill, cached by `buildRev` | Not saved. Each room gets a role from its furniture (bedroom, kitchen, workshop, infirmary, armory, hall). |

Rules:
- `src/sim` stays free of `three` and the DOM.
- Content lives in `src/data/house.ts`: floors, edge kinds, furniture, decor, defense attachments, balance.
- UI pushes commands: `paintFloor`, `buildEdge` (line draw), `setDoorState`, `placeFurniture`, `removeHouseItem`, `upgradeEdge`.
- All new randomness uses the seeded RNG.

## 4. Steps

Each step has a verify line. Run `npm test`, `npm run build`, and `npm run build:offline` at the end of each.

### Step 0. Stabilize
- Remove the M10 parallel `Room`, `WallModule`, and `rooms.ts` data. Keep the `lotRadius` and `roomTier` idea in the new balance table.
- Fix `applyCommands` structure. Fix the overlap bug by deleting the code it lives in.
- Verify: `tests/fullrun.test.ts` passes on seeds 2, 3, 5 again. Airship launches by day 10.

### Step 1. House layer in the simulation
- Floors, edges, flood fill rooms, `isIndoors(x, y)`.
- Placement rules with visible reasons: outside lot, hearth stage too low, floor needs a neighbor floor or the hearth, edge needs a floor on one side, furniture needs a floor, would seal a room with no door, would block the only door.
- Remove refunds half cost.
- Verify: Vitest cases for each rule and for the door reachability check.

### Step 2. Everyone builds
- One site concept. `jobs.ts` assigns idle colonists to the nearest unfinished floor, edge, or furniture, as it does for buildings. Builders stand on the open side of the piece.
- Build times in data. Floors fast, walls medium, furniture short.
- Verify: sim test where a bare lot gets walled, floored, and furnished by colonists with no player action after placing.

### Step 3. Movement through doors
- Today colonists walk straight lines. Add a small A star on the house lot only, using floors and door edges, cached by `buildRev`. Outside the lot, movement is unchanged.
- Closed walls and windows block colonists. Doors let them through. Doors are weaker than walls and are the point monsters aim for.
- Verify: a colonist in a back room reaches the outside through two doors. Sealing the last door shows an error instead of trapping people.

### Step 4. Living in the house (the Sims loop)
- Furniture has `use` slots: bed (rest), table and chairs (eat), sofa and rug (mingle), stove (cook, heat), shelf (storage), workbench (craft), sickbed (heal), armory rack (weapons), drafting table (airship parts).
- Day: colonists work at their jobs outside. Dusk: everyone walks home, eats at a table, then mingles or takes a post. Night: sleepers in beds, defenders at guns. Dawn: leave for work.
- Rest, healing, and warmth multipliers come from beds and room role. Mingle gives a small capped hope bonus. Decor (plants, paintings, rugs, lamps) adds a capped room comfort bonus.
- People inside a standing, enclosed room are safe. A breached room is not.
- Missing furniture shows a hint: "No free bed for Mara."
- Verify: tests for bed use, meal use, mingle hope gain, the dusk return, and the safe indoor rule.

### Step 5. Defense attached to the house
- **Gun ports.** An edge kind. A defender assigned to a port fires through it. Reuses the `nightDuty` and `guns` code in `combat.ts`.
- **Roof turrets.** Placed on roof pads. Two tiers, makeshift and heavy, with an upgrade path like the watchtower.
- **Wall lamps and spotlights.** Light sources that feed the existing light steps.
- **Wall upgrades.** Wood to reinforced to stone to metal, paid in resources, built by colonists.
- **Monsters.** Breakers attack house edges. The flow field counts house edges as cost, doors as the cheapest. Hunters still cannot hurt buildings.
- Keep the 8 real light limit. House lights use emissive materials and ground decals.
- Verify: tests that a port fires only with a defender, a turret damages and kills, a breached edge exposes occupants, and a stronger wall costs more to path through.

### Step 6. Perimeter and compound unchanged in feel
- Walls, gates, barricades, traps, lookout, and watchtower rules stay. They may not be placed on house floors. They may be placed right next to the lot.
- The scripted player keeps its outer rings, now outside the larger lot.
- Verify: full run still launches by day 10 on seeds 2, 3, 5, and 6.

### Step 7. Airship staging: the Moot and the Launch Pad
Story spine:
1. The survivors find the half ruined house and light the hearth.
2. They repair it and raise collective hope. The old owner's diary pages turn up as the house is repaired.
3. At hearth stage 3 with hope above a set level, the crew finds a sealed attic. Inside are the old owner's blueprints for a small balloon craft. The owner never finished it because the monsters came. This is why the house is the staging point and why no one left earlier.
4. **The Moot.** The crew votes on where to build. The player picks the Launch Pad site.

The Launch Pad:
- A 6 by 6 open air footprint (the 4 by 4 dock plus a 1 tile apron). The envelope inflates upward, so nothing roofed may sit above it.
- It must stay within 10 tiles of the house door, so the final dash on The Last Night is possible.
- It may sit inside the walls, touch the house lot, or sit outside the walls. Outside shows a clear exposure warning.
- Monsters treat the unfinished airship as a priority target. Its hp is the lose condition that exists today.

When the compound is cramped:
- **Site finder.** The Moot shows every valid spot as a green ghost, with a count of what is in the way.
- **Clear the Pad.** One click marks everything in the footprint for salvage. Colonists tear it down by day and the player gets 75 percent back.
- **Push Out the Wall.** If the pad needs more room, this tool draws a new wall line around it. Colonists build the new wall first and salvage the old one after, so there is never a gap. It costs materials and one working day.
- The worst case is always a few buildings lost, never a dead end.

House work:
- The Drafting Table furniture runs `buildComponent` work in the house. Components complete there.
- The Launch Pad hosts final assembly and launch.
- On The Last Night everyone shelters in the house. Fuel loads from house storage. In the final 30 seconds the colony runs from the door to the pad.
- The scripted full run auto picks the yard at the Moot.
- Not in M10.1: the Old Airfield hangar as a remote launch site. It would split the colony across two bases. Listed as a later milestone.
- Verify: tests for the unlock rule, site validation, Clear the Pad, Push Out the Wall with no gap, and a full launch.

### Step 8. Rendering
- Instanced floors and wall segments. Procedural low poly furniture in `src/render/meshes`, using the existing kit and palette. Warm inside, cold outside is preserved.
- Doors swing open when used.
- Roofs fade when a house item is selected or when the player holds Tab, so colonists inside can be seen. This closes the known issue "no indoor view".
- Verify: screenshots in `docs/screenshots/m10-1-*.jpg` of a bare lot, a furnished house, a night raid, and the cutaway.

### Step 9. Placement ghosts and rotation
- Replace the green and red box with a translucent copy of the real mesh. Green when valid, red when blocked, with a ground footprint outline and a pulse.
- Drag to draw floors and wall lines. Click to place single items.
- Rotation 0 to 3, not a boolean. R rotates, shift R rotates back, and the build panel has a rotate button for touch.
- The blocked reason shows next to the cursor, as section 9 of CLAUDE.md requires.
- Verify: manual check in the browser with screenshots.

### Step 10. Micro interactions
- Hover: soft glow on buildings, furniture, edges, colonists, and items. Done with emissive boost, using instance colors for instanced meshes. Cursor becomes a pointer.
- Click: short scale pulse, a ground ring, and a persistent selection outline.
- UI buttons get hover, press, and focus states.
- This code is render and UI only. It never writes sim state.
- Verify: manual check, plus a frame time check against section 15.6.

### Step 11. UI
- Build menu tabs: Structure (floor, wall, door, window), Furniture, Defense, Decor, Compound (the existing outside buildings).
- A room overlay names each detected room and warns "No door" or "No bed".
- Selection panel for furniture, edges, and rooms. Assignments for gun ports and turrets.
- Text never overflows. Panels never overlap the game view.

### Step 12. Save, docs, and close out
- Save version 4. A round trip test covers floors, edges, furniture, and in progress sites.
- Update `GAME_DESIGN.md` (sections 5.5, 7, 9, 11, 12, milestones with a "Done when"), `CHANGELOG.md`, `DECISIONS.md`, and `KNOWN_ISSUES.md`.
- Bump the version. All three commands pass.

## 5. Done when

A new player builds a floor and walls, adds a door, a bed, a table, and a stove, and sees colonists build each piece. They watch the colony leave at dawn, work outside, return at dusk, eat, mingle, and sleep. They add a gun port and a roof turret, survive a raid that breaks the outer wall, and stage the airship parts from the house. The scripted full run still launches by day 10 on seeds 2, 3, 5, and 6. All tests and both builds pass.

## 6. Risks

- **Pathing cost.** Keep the house A star confined to the lot and cache it. Check against the 10 tick budget.
- **Balance.** More defense can make nights trivial. Gun and wall numbers go in `src/data/house.ts` and are tuned with the scripted player.
- **Scope.** This is large. Suggested delivery in three parts so each can be reviewed:
  - **M10.1a:** Steps 0 to 3 (house layer, building, doors).
  - **M10.1b:** Steps 4 to 7 (living, defense, perimeter, airship).
  - **M10.1c:** Steps 8 to 12 (render, ghosts, micro interactions, UI, docs).
